# Captions and accessibility

## Captions are intentional
Ask whether captions are wanted when the request does not specify. Respect explicit instructions such as no subtitles or no text on screen. A caption file (SRT/VTT) can be delivered separately without burning it into the video.

## Timing and format
- Use UTF-8 text and sequential cue numbers for SRT.
- Use WebVTT timestamps and a `WEBVTT` header for VTT.
- Keep cue timing within the media duration; start times must precede end times.
- Split captions at natural phrase boundaries and avoid covering important visual action.
- Keep reading speed comfortable for the audience and language; shorten or split overly dense cues without changing meaning.
- Synchronize captions to the final narration edit, not an earlier script estimate.

## Readability
Use high contrast, a legible typeface, sufficient size, safe margins, and consistent placement. Avoid placing captions over faces, product details, recipe steps, or essential UI. Test on a phone-sized preview as well as the full frame.

## Accessibility checks
- Provide meaningful descriptions for essential visual information when requested.
- Do not rely on color alone to communicate meaning.
- Keep dialogue distinguishable from music and effects.
- Avoid flashing patterns and unnecessarily rapid visual changes.
- Ensure titles and graphics remain on screen long enough to read.
- For multilingual delivery, review translation, idiom, line breaks, and timing with a fluent speaker where possible.

## Delivery
When requested, deliver SRT and/or VTT separately and state whether captions are burned in or sidecar files. Never claim accessibility compliance based only on automated checks.
