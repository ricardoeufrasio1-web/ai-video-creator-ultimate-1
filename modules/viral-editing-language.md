# Dynamic editing and attention design

## Purpose
Build engaging, legible edits for any niche and duration. "Viral" is an editorial objective, never a guaranteed outcome or a substitute for audience research.

## Editing grammar
1. Open on the strongest visual, result, question, conflict, or action. Avoid generic intros unless the format requires them.
2. Establish a clear promise or question early.
3. Use a beat map: hook, setup, escalation, proof/demo, payoff, close/CTA. Long-form may use multiple nested arcs.
4. Change shot size, composition, graphic state, or information layer when attention or comprehension benefits. Do not add arbitrary cuts just to increase cut count.
5. Use jump cuts, J-cuts, L-cuts, match cuts, inserts, reaction shots, punch-ins, reframing, split screens, picture-in-picture, speed ramps, freeze frames, whip pans, wipes, dissolves, graphic transitions, animated masks, parallax, and montage when appropriate.
6. Synchronize cuts, motion, typography and sound design to narration, action, musical beats, and narrative emphasis.
7. Use silence and slower holds deliberately; constant stimulation causes fatigue.
8. Deliver a payoff that resolves the opening promise. End with a natural loop or clear CTA only when suitable.

## Motion graphics and typography
- Kinetic typography, word-by-word emphasis, title cards, lower thirds, labels, arrows, diagrams, maps, charts, UI overlays, callouts, progress indicators, counters, timelines, subtitles, captions, end cards, branded frames.
- Preserve safe areas, contrast, readable duration, line length, font licensing and language-specific typography.
- Never place critical information only in color or fast flashing visuals. Avoid seizure-inducing flashing.

## Sound design
Use dialogue/narration as the intelligibility anchor. Layer ambience, room tone, music, foley, transitions, impacts, risers and accents with controlled gain and fades. Duck music under speech when supported. Avoid clipping, abrupt edits, unlicensed music and excessive loudness.

## Timing and retention
- Short-form: earn attention immediately, remove dead air, vary visual information, and pay off quickly.
- Long-form: use chapters, resets, examples, questions, visual evidence, recaps and narrative progression. Do not force short-form pacing across an entire long video.
- Make every cut answer "what new information or feeling does this add?"
- Optimize for comprehension, retention and audience trust rather than deceptive clickbait.

## Accessibility and trust
Keep captions accurate and synchronized when requested; preserve readable contrast and pacing. Do not fabricate evidence, testimonials, metrics, news footage or before/after results. Label illustrative reconstructions where viewers could mistake them for documentary evidence.
